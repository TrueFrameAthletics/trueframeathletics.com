What does mental toughness mean at TrueFrame?

Mental toughness is the practice of returning to a useful action when a difficult moment pulls your attention away from the game. An athlete can feel disappointed and still communicate, listen to instruction, and carry out the next responsibility.

TrueFrame’s coaching focuses on those responses. We help athletes recognize what carries over from a mistake, rehearse a brief reset, and choose an action within their control. We do not ask athletes to hide distress or play through an injury.

How the training becomes real-time support.

We begin by learning which situations challenge the athlete: a turnover, a missed opportunity, correction from a coach, or a change in playing time. In practice, Richard helps the athlete rehearse a response using FRAME: Find It, Reset, Anchor, Move, Execute.

During approved competition moments, a short sideline check-in reinforces that familiar response. The athlete identifies what the coach needs next. Richard observes the follow-through and uses it to guide further practice.

What might an athlete work on?

  • Returning attention to the next assignment after a mistake.
  • Communicating with teammates when frustrated.
  • Listening to correction and identifying the requested action.
  • Staying ready to contribute after time on the bench.
  • Using a practiced cue when pressure increases.

These are coaching goals, not promised results. We consider the athlete’s own account alongside observable behavior, rather than assuming we know how they feel from body language.

Questions from athletes, parents, and coaches.

Is mental toughness the same as confidence?

At TrueFrame, confidence is one part of the conversation. We also practice useful actions an athlete can take when confidence is low, so their next response does not depend on feeling certain.

Do you coach during games?

Yes. Real-time support is central to the offering. Sideline access, timing, and communication are coordinated with coaching staff and subject to event rules.

Is this sports psychology treatment?

TrueFrame provides performance coaching and mental skills practice. It does not provide clinical assessment, psychotherapy, or mental-health treatment. Richard works alongside existing staff within that scope.

Where is TrueFrame based?

TrueFrame is based in Omaha, Nebraska. Discuss your location and schedule with Richard to establish whether an in-person engagement fits.
