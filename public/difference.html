The TrueFrame Difference

We train what happens after the mistake.

A missed shot. A costly turnover. A role change. A difficult loss. TrueFrame Athletics helps athletes develop a practiced response for the moments when competition doesn't go as planned.

We connect competition-film observations, the FRAME Reset Method, realistic pressure scenarios, and follow-up feedback so athletes have something specific to do next—not just something positive to think.

01 / What we do

Turn mental-performance concepts into actions athletes can practice.

TrueFrame specializes in applied adversity-response coaching across sports. We focus on behaviors athletes can control when frustration, hesitation, pressure, or uncertainty shows up.

01 / OBSERVE

Study real competition moments

With appropriate access to film, we identify observable changes after mistakes: body language, communication, hesitation, decision-making, and re-engagement. Film gives us a concrete starting point—not a diagnosis of what an athlete feels.

02 / RESET

Teach the FRAME response

Athletes practice Find It, Reset, Anchor, Move, Execute: recognize what happened, interrupt an unhelpful reaction, return attention to a cue, re-engage, and commit to the next action.

03 / REHEARSE

Create realistic pressure scenarios

We use sport-relevant situations—missed opportunities, reduced playing time, late-game pressure, and momentum swings—to rehearse a specific next-play response before the stakes are real.

04 / REVIEW

Track observable development

We use available competition film, athlete reflection, and coach feedback to discuss what changed. We look for behaviors such as quicker re-engagement, clearer communication, and commitment to the next assignment.

02 / Why it helps

Knowing what to do is different from practicing it.

Under pressure, a reminder to “be confident” may not give an athlete a usable next action. Rehearsal and feedback create an opportunity to build that action into a repeatable routine.

FOR ATHLETES

A plan for the next moment

Practice returning attention to controllable actions after mistakes, setbacks, frustration, or a drop in confidence.

FOR COACHES

Specific cues to reinforce

Use observable behaviors—communicate, recover position, prepare for the next assignment—instead of relying only on general encouragement.

FOR TEAMS & SCHOOLS

Training that fits the sport

Bring mental-performance application into workshops and team development, alongside existing coaches and support staff.

Our goal: Help athletes develop more consistent response habits over time. We do not promise perfect games, guaranteed results, or a specific improvement from any single workshop.
03 / What it looks like

A missed play. A trainable next play.

Here's an illustrative example of how TrueFrame turns a difficult competition moment into a coaching plan.

THE MOMENT

An athlete turns the ball over.

The athlete drops their head, stays frustrated, and is late getting back into position. The next play is affected—not just the original mistake.

What we identify: Head position, re-engagement, communication, and next-assignment behavior.

THE RESPONSE WE PRACTICE

FRAME in action.

  • Find It: Acknowledge the turnover.
  • Reset: Break the frustration loop.
  • Anchor: Use a short cue such as “Next assignment.”
  • Move: Recover position and communicate.
  • Execute: Commit to the next defensive play.
What we review: In later practices or available competition film, we look for observable next-play responses and use those observations to refine the training plan.
04 / Our process

Film. Practice. Compete. Review.

TrueFrame provides more than a speech or a one-time reminder. The aim is to build a process an athlete and their coaches can keep using.

1. Identify the challenge

Define the pressure moment and observable response to improve.

2. Build a FRAME routine

Choose a practical cue and next-play action for the athlete's sport.

3. Rehearse under pressure

Repeat the response in realistic, sport-specific scenarios.

4. Revisit the evidence

Use film and feedback, when available, to adjust the next training step.

05 / How we're different

Different specialties. Complementary strengths.

Sport psychology, mental-performance consulting, mindfulness coaching, and athletic coaching all play important roles. TrueFrame's distinction is the way our service combines film-guided adversity observations, the FRAME routine, pressure rehearsal, and behavioral follow-up.

TRUEFRAME ATHLETICS

Applied adversity-response training

We help athletes rehearse specific responses to mistakes, pressure, role changes, and setbacks. Our coaching connects competition observation with a simple reset method, realistic scenarios, and follow-up on observable behavior.

Sport & performance psychology

This field addresses focus, confidence, motivation, preparation, coping skills, performance, and psychological well-being. Qualified licensed professionals may also provide clinical care within their scope.

Mental-performance consultants

These specialists teach and apply mental skills, including attention, imagery, self-regulation, and preparation. Certified Mental Performance Consultants (CMPCs) may use practical exercises and sport-based observations.

Mindfulness & meditation coaches

These approaches often develop present-moment awareness, attention, breathing, and acceptance. Such skills can support an athlete's reset and anchor.

Psychologists & therapists

Appropriately licensed clinicians can assess and treat mental-health concerns that are outside TrueFrame's performance-coaching scope.

We do not claim others never use film, pressure drills, or mental-skills training. Our difference is our defined adversity-response focus and how we bring those methods together into one applied service.
