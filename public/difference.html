The TrueFrame Difference

The mistake is a moment. The response is a skill.

A missed shot. A costly turnover. A role change. A difficult loss. TrueFrame Athletics helps athletes develop a practiced response for the moments when competition doesn't go as planned.

TrueFrame Athletics provides mental-performance and adversity-response coaching for athletes across all sports. Our approach adapts to the specific challenges of each sport and athlete.

01 / What makes TrueFrame different

Four parts. One practiced response.

TrueFrame turns mental-performance concepts into actions athletes can rehearse. We focus on behaviors an athlete can control when frustration, hesitation, pressure, or uncertainty shows up.

A · Competition film

Competition-film observations

We identify observable performance habits after mistakes, such as hesitation, communication, composure, re-engagement, and next-play decisions.

What we observe after a mistake

  • Hesitation
  • Communication
  • Composure
  • Re-engagement
  • Next-play decisions

Film gives coaching a concrete starting point. It is not a diagnosis of what an athlete feels.

B · FRAME

FRAME Reset Method

A simple, repeatable five-step response that athletes rehearse until it is ready when a difficult moment arrives.

The five steps

  • Find It — Recognize the moment.
  • Reset — Interrupt an unhelpful reaction.
  • Anchor — Refocus on something controllable.
  • Move — Re-engage.
  • Execute — Commit to the next action.
C · Pressure training

Realistic pressure training

We rehearse responses to mistakes, setbacks, confidence loss, role changes, and pressure in sport-specific situations.

Situations we rehearse

  • Mistakes
  • Setbacks
  • Confidence loss
  • Role changes
  • Pressure

Scenarios are built around the athlete's own sport and role, so the response is practiced in a situation that feels familiar.

D · Follow-up

Follow-up development

We use available film, athlete reflection, and coach observations to review targeted behaviors and refine the training plan.

What we review

  • Available film
  • The athlete's own reflection
  • Coach observations

Together these show which behaviors are changing and what the next training step should be. We do not promise specific results.

02 / Why it helps

Knowing what to do is different from practicing it.

Under pressure, a reminder to “be confident” may not give an athlete a usable next action. Rehearsal and feedback create an opportunity to build that action into a repeatable routine.

FOR ATHLETES

A plan for the next moment

Practice returning attention to controllable actions after mistakes, setbacks, frustration, or a drop in confidence.

FOR COACHES

Specific cues to reinforce

Use observable behaviors—communicate, recover position, prepare for the next assignment—instead of relying only on general encouragement.

FOR TEAMS & SCHOOLS

Training that fits the sport

Bring mental-performance application into workshops and team development, alongside existing coaches and support staff.

Our goal: Help athletes develop more consistent response habits over time. We do not promise perfect games, guaranteed results, or a specific improvement from any single workshop.
03 / What it looks like

A costly mistake. A trainable next play.

An illustrative example of how TrueFrame turns a difficult competition moment into a coaching plan. The same process applies across sports: a dropped pass, a missed serve, a fumbled grounder, a false start.

The moment

An athlete turns the ball over.

It is a costly turnover late in a close game. The athlete drops their head, stays frustrated, and is late getting back into position. The next play is affected, not just the original mistake.

The response we practice

FRAME in action.

  • Find It: Acknowledge the turnover.
  • Reset: Interrupt the frustration with one breath and a reset cue.
  • Anchor: Use a short cue such as “Next play.”
  • Move: Get back into position and communicate.
  • Execute: Commit to the next assignment.
What we look for

On the next play.

  • How quickly the athlete re-engages
  • Head position and body language
  • Communication with teammates
  • Decisiveness on the next decision
  • Effort and positioning on the next assignment
What we review: In later practices or available competition film, we look for observable next-play responses and use those observations to refine the training plan. See what a development report can look like.
04 / How TrueFrame compares

Different specialties. Complementary strengths.

Sport psychology, mental-performance consulting, mindfulness coaching, and psychology all play important roles, and other qualified professionals may also use film and pressure exercises. TrueFrame's distinction is the particular combination of adversity-response coaching, the FRAME Reset Method, film-guided observations, rehearsals, and feedback.

TrueFrame Athletics

Applied adversity-response coaching

We help athletes rehearse specific responses to mistakes, pressure, role changes, and setbacks. Our coaching connects competition observation with a simple reset method, realistic scenarios, and follow-up on observable behavior.

Sport psychology

This field addresses focus, confidence, motivation, preparation, coping skills, performance, and psychological well-being. Qualified licensed professionals may also provide clinical care within their scope.

Mental-performance consulting

These specialists teach and apply mental skills, including attention, imagery, self-regulation, and preparation. Certified Mental Performance Consultants (CMPCs) may use practical exercises and sport-based observations.

Mindfulness coaching

These approaches often develop present-moment awareness, attention, breathing, and acceptance. Such skills can support an athlete's reset and anchor.

Licensed psychologists

Appropriately licensed clinicians can assess and treat mental-health concerns that are outside TrueFrame's performance-coaching scope.

We do not claim others never use film, pressure drills, or mental-skills training. Our difference is the defined adversity-response focus and how we bring these methods together into one applied service.
Built to work alongside your staff

A complement to your coaches and support staff.

TrueFrame Athletics doesn't replace sports psychologists, mental-performance professionals, or athletic coaches. We work alongside existing staff to reinforce performance skills through practical, observable actions.

What coaches keep doing

Leading tactics, technique, playing roles, training plans, and team standards.

What TrueFrame contributes

Structured next-play routines, adversity-response scenarios, film-guided discussion, and feedback on the behaviors being trained.

Clear scope: TrueFrame provides performance coaching and athlete development, not clinical diagnosis, psychotherapy, or mental-health treatment. Clinical concerns belong with appropriately licensed professionals.
