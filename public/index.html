What we believe

Mistakes happen. The response can be trained.

No athlete avoids mistakes, pressure, or setbacks. What shapes the next play is the response, and a response is something an athlete can practice.

Mistakes are part of sport

Turnovers, misses, errors, and setbacks happen to every athlete. What matters most is what happens in the seconds that follow.

The response is a skill

Resetting, refocusing, and committing to the next action can be learned and rehearsed like any other part of performance.

Training should look like competition

Realistic pressure scenarios, film observations, and coach input help athletes build responses they can use when it counts.

The TrueFrame Difference

We Don't Just Talk About Mental Performance. We Train It.

TrueFrame Athletics helps athletes across sports develop practical, repeatable responses to mistakes, pressure, confidence loss, role changes, and adversity. Our FRAME system gives athletes tools they can rehearse and use when competition becomes difficult.

01 / IDENTIFY

Understand the Challenge

Identify the situations that interrupt confidence, composure, communication, and decision-making. When available, game film helps reveal observable performance patterns.

The method

The FRAME Reset Method

Competition will never be perfect. The goal is not to eliminate difficult moments. The goal is to develop a better response to them.

F

Find It

Recognize what happened without allowing the moment to control what happens next.

R

Reset

Interrupt the negative emotional or mental response.

A

Anchor

Return attention to a controllable cue, action, breath, word, assignment, or responsibility.

M

Move

Physically and mentally re-engage with the competition.

E

Execute

Make the next decision with commitment.

Observable behaviors

What We Look For

TrueFrame observes coaching and performance markers—not psychological diagnoses.

When competition film is available, it shows what actually happens after a mistake: the hesitation, the body language, the communication, and how quickly the athlete re-engages. Those observations become part of the athlete's development plan, and we return to film and coach observations to see whether the behaviors are changing.

Reset Speed

How quickly does the athlete mentally and behaviorally recover after a mistake?

Response to Adversity

Does a difficult moment affect the next play, possession, rep, or decision?

Decision Trust

Does the athlete recognize an opportunity and commit to the decision, or hesitate?

Competitive Composure

How does the athlete respond when pressure increases?

Body-Language Recovery

What happens physically after mistakes, setbacks, or frustration?

Re-engagement

How quickly does the athlete return to productive competition?

Communication

Does communication disappear or improve when the athlete experiences adversity?

Focus

Can the athlete return attention to the present play instead of remaining stuck on the previous one?

Execution Under Pressure

Can the athlete apply their training when the situation becomes more difficult?

TrueFrame does not promise perfect performances. We help athletes develop better responses and more consistent performance behaviors.

How to get started

Four simple steps.

Whether it's one athlete, a team, or a whole program, getting started is straightforward.

Reach out

Tell us about the athlete or team, the sport, and what you're seeing in competition.

Talk it through

We discuss goals, the moments that are hardest, and whether individual coaching, a workshop, or a team program fits best.

We shape a practical plan around the sport, the schedule, and any available film or coach observations.
