How the process works

Observe. Plan. Review.

Every report follows the same three stages, so athletes and coaches can see what was observed, what is being practiced, and what changed when we looked again.

Stage 1

Initial observation

We review available film, athlete reflection, and coach observations to describe what happens after mistakes and setbacks.

Stage 2

Targeted practice plan

We build a short plan around the behaviors that matter most: FRAME steps, a personal cue, and sport-specific pressure scenarios.

Stage 3

Follow-up observation

We look at the same behaviors again, describe what we see, and refine the plan. We do not promise specific results.

Illustrative scenario

Sample Athlete competes hard in practice. In close games, a mistake tends to carry into the next play: the athlete looks frustrated, hesitates on the next decision, and communicates later than usual. The coach wants the athlete to have a reliable way to reset. This fictional scenario shows how TrueFrame describes what it sees, builds a practice plan around it, and looks at the same behaviors again afterward.

“Next play” stands in for the next point, possession, pitch, attempt, or position, so the same format can be adapted to any sport.

01

Response after mistakes

Initial observation

In the sample film, the athlete drops their head and slows down after a turnover. The next assignment starts late, and frustration is visible in their body language.

Targeted practice plan

Rehearse the first FRAME steps, Find It and Reset, with one short cue. Practice them in scenarios that recreate a costly mistake late in a close game.

Follow-up observation

In later sample film, the athlete more often uses the cue and keeps their head up. After repeated errors the response is still slower, so it remains a focus.

02

Re-engagement after setbacks

Initial observation

After a missed opportunity or a swing in momentum, the athlete takes time to get back into the flow. Effort stays steady, but attention appears to stay on what just happened.

Targeted practice plan

Practice Anchor and Move: refocus on one controllable responsibility, then re-engage physically. Use short drills that start right after a simulated setback.

Follow-up observation

Sample follow-up film shows the athlete reconnecting with a controllable assignment sooner in several situations. Larger momentum swings are still developing, so they stay on the plan.

03

Communication under pressure

Initial observation

When the team is behind or a mistake has just happened, the athlete grows quieter and communicates later than they do in practice.

Targeted practice plan

Agree on a few short calls for pressure moments and rehearse them in small-group and team scenarios until they become automatic.

Follow-up observation

Sample coach notes describe clearer, earlier communication during tense stretches. It is steadier in some situations and still inconsistent in others.

04

Hesitation and decision-making

Initial observation

On the play after a mistake, the athlete hesitates before committing and tends to choose the safest option instead of the best one.

Targeted practice plan

Pair the Execute step with a pre-decided next action, then rehearse decisions in realistic, sport-specific pressure scenarios with feedback after each repetition.

Follow-up observation

Sample film shows quicker, more decisive choices in familiar scenarios. In unfamiliar situations the athlete still pauses, so scenarios are broadened.

05

Application of reset cues

Initial observation

The athlete understands what a reset is but has no consistent cue. Reactions vary from one mistake to the next.

Targeted practice plan

Choose a personal reset cue with the athlete, such as a word, a breath, or a physical action that fits their sport. Practice it until it can be used without stopping play.

Follow-up observation

In sample observations, the athlete uses the cue more consistently in practice and in some game moments. Using it in the highest-pressure moments is the next thing to practice.

06

Coach and athlete observations

Initial observation

The coach sees an athlete who plays well in practice but shows more frustration in games. The athlete reports that their mind stays on the last play.

Targeted practice plan

Collect brief coach notes after games and short athlete reflections, both tied to the same behaviors named in the practice plan, so everyone is looking at the same things.

Follow-up observation

Sample coach notes mention steadier composure after mistakes. The athlete's reflection says the cue helps and names disputed calls as the hardest situation to reset from.

07

Next development steps

Initial observation

After the first review, the athlete understands FRAME and has a personal cue. The cue is not yet reliable in the highest-pressure moments.

Targeted practice plan

Continue the training cycle with more demanding pressure scenarios, agree on a point for the next film review, and confirm with the coach that the cues match team language.

Follow-up observation

At the next review, the same behaviors are observed again: response after mistakes, re-engagement, communication, decision-making, and use of the cue. The plan is then updated based on what is seen.

Sample format only. Observations describe coachable behaviors, not psychological assessments, and this sample uses descriptions rather than scores or percentages.

About this sample. This is a fictional scenario created to show how a report can be organized. It does not describe a real athlete, team, or engagement, and it is not evidence of results or typical outcomes. The follow-up notes illustrate the format only. TrueFrame provides performance coaching and athlete development, not clinical diagnosis, psychotherapy, or mental-health treatment.
