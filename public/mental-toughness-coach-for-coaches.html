What does mental toughness mean here?

A missed shot, turnover, or lost point can carry into the next opportunity. TrueFrame treats mental toughness as a set of trainable responses: noticing the moment, refocusing on a controllable responsibility, and committing to the next action.

The aim is not to suppress emotion or demand that athletes ignore difficulty. It is to give athletes and coaches a practical response they can rehearse when pressure rises. Adversity is inevitable. Your response is trainable.

Coach education and team communication workshops

Staff workshops focus on what to say and do immediately after an athlete makes a mistake. Coaches rehearse concise instructions, agree on shared cues, and plan how to reinforce those cues without interrupting practice.

Illustrative basketball scenario: A guard turns the ball over and slows down while arguing about the play. Instead of repeating “focus,” the coach uses a cue practiced beforehand: “Reset. Find your assignment.” The athlete resets, locates the defensive responsibility, communicates, and re-engages. The staff reviews the response afterward rather than assuming one cue will work for every athlete.

The same process can be adapted to a missed serve, dropped pass, or difficult race segment. Explore coach workshop details.

The five steps of the FRAME Reset Method

  1. Find It: Recognize the moment and your response.
  2. Reset: Interrupt unhelpful thinking or frustration.
  3. Anchor: Refocus on a controllable cue or responsibility.
  4. Move: Re-engage physically and mentally.
  5. Execute: Commit to the next decision and action.

Coaches and athletes share the framework while adapting cues to the individual, sport, and role. Practice makes the cue meaningful before a coach uses it from the sideline. See how FRAME fits into training.

How film review and season support work

Film gives staff and athletes another perspective on what happens after a mistake. We identify a behavior to practice, rehearse an appropriate response, and revisit that behavior in later observations. Film can show actions and communication; it cannot establish an athlete’s internal state or diagnose a psychological condition.

For example, a staff may track whether an athlete resumes their assignment after an error and communicates on the following possession. Athlete reflection adds context to what coaches see. If film is unavailable, training can start with athlete reflection and coach observations.

Questions to ask before hiring a coach

  • Will athletes and staff practice responses in realistic scenarios?
  • How will the work fit our sport, roles, and practice schedule?
  • What observable behaviors will we review over time?
  • What follow-up, deliverables, and fees are included?
  • How does the coach work alongside our existing support staff?

TrueFrame provides mental performance coaching and athlete development, not psychotherapy, clinical diagnosis, or mental-health treatment. We complement existing coaches, counselors, and sport psychology professionals.

Frequently asked questions

Is this only for head coaches?

No. Head coaches, assistants, athletic directors, and support staff can participate. Shared language helps everyone reinforce the same response.

Can we start with one athlete?

Yes. Individual coaching builds a routine around the athlete’s sport, role, and goals.

Are virtual sessions available?

Individual coaching and film review can be delivered virtually. Team and staff workshop formats are confirmed before booking.
