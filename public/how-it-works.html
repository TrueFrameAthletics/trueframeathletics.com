THE FRAME RESET METHOD

FRAME gives a short check-in a clear direction.

Athletes learn the method in practice. During competition, Richard can reinforce a familiar cue at an appropriate break rather than introduce a long lesson.

01

Find It

Recognize the mistake or pressure moment without letting it define the next one.

02

Reset

Interrupt the reaction with a brief, practiced reset.

03

Anchor

Bring attention to a controllable cue, assignment, or responsibility.

04

Move

Re-engage physically and mentally with the team and competition.

05

Execute

Commit to the next decision and action.

ILLUSTRATIVE BASKETBALL EXAMPLE

You’re adjusting your lineup. A player on the bench is still replaying their last mistake.

At an agreed moment, Richard checks in, helps the athlete use a brief FRAME reset, and brings their attention back to your instruction.

“What does Coach need from you when you go back in?”

THE NEXT OPPORTUNITY

One clear action.

Get back on defense. Communicate. Execute the assignment.

Richard watches for that response, reinforces the follow-through, and uses what happens next to guide future practice.

IN THE MOMENT

What we observe

We look for visible actions: returning to the assignment, communicating again, listening to instruction, and following through on the agreed next action. Athlete reflection adds context; body language alone does not tell us what someone is thinking.
